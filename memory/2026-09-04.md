## 0.8.0: uninstall, optimize, status --watch

The three parity items deferred from 0.7.0. Two of them landed narrower than
they were asked for, and the narrowing is the interesting part.

`uninstall` cannot delete, and that is structural rather than a preference.
`safety::is_protected` refuses `/Applications` and everything beneath it, and
refuses everything under `~/Library` outside a four-entry allowlist — a boundary
the repository's own property test asserts
(`is_protected(home/"Library/Application Support")`). Deleting an app bundle and
its support files needs that list widened for every code path, not just this
one. So the command does the half a person cannot do by hand: resolve the bundle
identifier and find what macOS keys to it. That half is worth having — Amazon
Kindle is `com.amazon.Lassen`, so a name-matching uninstaller finds none of its
files and sweeps everything containing the word.

`optimize` is three tasks, not twenty-two. Root-requiring and hours-long tasks
stay out. DNS was in the first version and came out: on modern macOS the
resolver cache lives in `mDNSResponder`, so `dscacheutil -flushcache` would have
reported success while leaving the cache it named intact.

## Reviews found seven, then three more

Every finding was real, and most were the same failure: a claim outrunning the
implementation.

- `uninstall` promised "every file belonging to an app" while matching only
  identifier-named paths. VS Code keeps `~/Library/Application Support/Code`,
  which the algorithm cannot reach. The contract is now "conservative report,
  not inventory" in six places.
- Group-container matching was removed rather than narrowed. Names come from an
  arbitrary `com.apple.security.application-groups` entitlement: here
  `2BUA8C4S2C.com.1password` belongs to an app identified
  `com.1password.1password`, and 49 of 171 containers begin with `group.` and
  carry no identifier at all. A suffix rule both missed real containers and
  could attribute another app's data to this one.
- `optimize --apply` with no `--task` still ran everything behind one prompt,
  because `plan_danger` takes the maximum. That is exactly what `--task` was
  added to prevent, and it was visible in the command's own preview.
- `status --watch` joined a worker that only checks its stop flag between
  samples, so `q` waited out an in-flight probe and a hung command would have
  blocked exit forever. The comment claiming otherwise was false.
- The battery row said "none" for both a battery-less Mac and a failed probe,
  asserting hardware that nothing established.
- The README's own primary example passed `--task dns` after DNS was removed.

## What the reviews could not have caught

Two empirical claims in the review did not reproduce and were not acted on.
`ps -Axo comm=` does not truncate here — longest line is 220 characters with and
without `ww` — though `ww` was added anyway since it costs nothing. Measure
before accepting a finding, the same as before fixing one.
